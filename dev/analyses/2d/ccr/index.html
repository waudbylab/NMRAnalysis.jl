Cross-correlated relaxation (CCR)

Cross-correlated relaxation between two mechanisms, such as the ¹H–¹⁵N dipolar coupling and the ¹⁵N CSA, reports on bond orientation and dynamics. ccr2d measures the CCR rate η from spectra in which a coherence decays or builds up over a relaxation period $T$:

\[\tanh(\eta T) = \frac{I_\text{buildup}}{I_\text{decay}}.\]

With symmetric reconversion, two buildup and two decay spectra cancel the effect of auto-relaxation:

\[\tanh(\eta T) = \sqrt{\frac{I_{\text{bu},1} \cdot I_{\text{bu},2}}{I_{\text{dec},1} \cdot I_{\text{dec},2}}}.\]

Usage

Give the decay spectra, then the buildup, and the relaxation period T in seconds:

using NMRAnalysis
ccr2d(1, 2; T=0.08)                # one decay, one buildup
ccr2d([1, 4], [2, 3]; T=0.08)      # symmetric reconversion

Without T, you're asked for it.

Example

Symmetric reconversion on the calreticulin C-terminal domain at 1.2 GHz, with $T = 40$ ms:

decay, buildup = example("ccr2d_calr")
ccr2d(decay, buildup; T=0.04)
