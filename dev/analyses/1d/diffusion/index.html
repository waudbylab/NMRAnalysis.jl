Diffusion

diffusion1d fits the integral of each region against gradient strength with the Stejskal-Tanner equation,

\[I(g) = A \exp \left( -\left[ \gamma\delta\sigma g G_\mathrm{max} \right]^2 \left[\Delta - \delta/3 \right] D \right),\]

where $g$ is the relative gradient strength, $\delta$ the gradient pulse length, $\Delta$ the diffusion delay, $\sigma$ the gradient shape factor and $\gamma$ the gyromagnetic ratio. The hydrodynamic radius then follows from the Stokes-Einstein relation,

\[D = \frac{k_B T}{6\pi \eta r_H},\]

with the solvent viscosity $\eta$ at the sample temperature from Cho et al., J. Phys. Chem. B 103, 1991 (1999).

Usage

Bruker doesn't store the gradient ramp, so give it as relative strengths from 0 to 1, one per spectrum:

using NMRAnalysis
diffusion1d("106"; gradients=0.05:0.1:0.95)

Without gradients, you're asked for the first and last strengths, in percent, of a linear ramp: the values you typed into TopSpin's dosy dialogue.

The other parameters are read from the acquisition parameters and offered for you to confirm or change:

Gradient pulse length δ = 4000.0 µs (2*p30). Press enter to accept, or type a new value:
Diffusion delay Δ = 0.1 s (d20). Press enter to accept, or type a new value:

The solvent and temperature come from the acquisition parameters. The hydrodynamic radius is reported for D2O and H2O+D2O only.

Example

An XSTE experiment on α-synuclein:

diffusion1d(example("diffusion1d_asyn"); gradients=0.05:0.1:0.95)
