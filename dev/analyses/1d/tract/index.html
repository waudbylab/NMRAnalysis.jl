TRACT

TRACT (Lee et al., J. Magn. Reson. 178, 72 (2006)) estimates the rotational correlation time τc of a protein from the relaxation rates of the TROSY and anti-TROSY components of the amide ¹⁵N doublet. Each is fitted as an exponential decay, and the difference gives the cross-correlated relaxation rate,

\[\eta_{xy} = \frac{R_\mathrm{anti} - R_\mathrm{TROSY}}{2} = f \left[ 4 J(0) + 3 J(\omega_N) \right], \qquad J(\omega) = \frac{2}{5} \frac{\tau_c}{1 + \omega^2\tau_c^2},\]

which is solved for τc. $f$ holds the dipolar and CSA constants: an N–H bond length of 1.02 Å, a ¹⁵N CSA of 160 ppm, and an angle of 17° between the two tensors.

TRACT assumes a rigid molecule that tumbles isotropically. Internal motion lowers η, so τc is an effective value, and a lower bound for a flexible protein.

Usage

Give the TROSY experiment, then the anti-TROSY:

using NMRAnalysis
tract("12", "13")

Both share one integration region, which starts on the amide envelope from 7.5 to 9.5 ppm. The delays are read from each experiment's vdlist, or give them with T:

tract("12", "13"; T=[0.0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04, 0.08])

Example

TRACT on calreticulin:

trosy, antitrosy = example("tract_calr")
tract(trosy, antitrosy)
