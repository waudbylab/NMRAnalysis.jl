Pulse calibration

calibration1d analyses a nutation experiment, a series of spectra with increasing pulse length, to give the B₁ field strength, the 90° pulse length and the B₁ inhomogeneity. Use it to calibrate spin-lock powers for R1ρ and CEST.

The integral is fitted to a sinusoid with a Gaussian envelope,

\[I(t) = A \sin(2\pi \nu t) \exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}(2\pi \sigma \nu t)^2\right), \qquad t_{90} = \frac{1}{4\nu},\]

or a cosine for an experiment that starts from transverse magnetisation. A Gaussian distribution of B₁ with relative width σ produces exactly this envelope, so σ is the B₁ inhomogeneity. Relaxation during the pulse also damps the signal, so σ is an upper bound, and least reliable at low power, where the pulses are longest.

With several experiments at different powers, the fields are fitted to

\[\nu_1(p) = \nu_1^\text{ref} \cdot 10^{-L(\text{dB}(p) - \text{dB}(p_\text{ref}))/20},\]

where a linearity $L$ of 1 is an ideal amplifier, and less than 1 means the field falls short at high power.

Usage

using NMRAnalysis
calibration1d("1")                 # one power level
calibration1d(["1", "2", "3"])     # several, for the calibration curve

The pulse lengths, the power and the modulation are read from the pulse-sequence annotations, or asked for:

KeywordMeaningAnnotation
TPulse lengths, scalibration.duration
powerPower of each experiment, dBcalibration.power
phase:sine or :cosinecalibration.model

An annotated ¹⁹F nutation sequence is available as 19f_calib_nut.cw. Its nominal flip angle runs to 720°: measuring σ needs several periods of nutation.

Example

A ¹⁹F calibration at two power levels:

calibration1d(example("calibration1d_19f"))

Use the calibration

B1Calibration turns the results into a calibration for the exchange analyses, or to work out powers and fields:

cal = B1Calibration(calibration1d(["1", "2", "3"]))

hz(Power(-15.0, :dB), cal)     # the field at a power
Power(2000.0, cal)             # the power for a 2 kHz field

exchange1d(["11", "12"]; calibration=cal)

exchange1d and setupR1rhopowers also take the calibration experiments directly, and fit them without a window:

exchange1d(["11", "12"]; calibration=["1", "2", "3"])
setupR1rhopowers(["1", "2", "3"])

When that analysis is saved, the calibration is saved with it, in calibration/. See the R1ρ tutorial for setting up spin-lock powers.
